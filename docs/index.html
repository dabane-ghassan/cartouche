A Dynamic Island for GNOME.

The clock in the top bar becomes a living pill: what is playing, volume and brightness, notifications, charging, headphones, and a glance at your day.

curl -fsSL https://dabane-ghassan.github.io/cartouche/install.sh | sh

Paste it in a terminal, then log out and back in once. Version 1.0.3, free and open source, for GNOME 45 to 51 on any distro.

🎵

Now playing

Album art and bars around the clock, in the colour of the cover. Open it for the player: seek, skip, pause. Any app that speaks MPRIS.

🔊

Volume and brightness

GNOME's pop-up in the middle of the screen moves up into the island. Keyboard light and microphone too, as a bar or sixteen segments.

💬

Notifications

They drop into the island instead of a banner, and still land in GNOME's list. Click to open, swipe up to put away.

🔌

Charging, headphones

Plugging in, a full or low battery, AirPods and other Bluetooth devices connecting, with their battery.

📅

Your day

Open it for the week, your next events from GNOME's calendar, battery, and a Do Not Disturb switch.

🔒

Lock screen

What is playing, and volume and brightness, stay on the lock screen. Notifications and your calendar never do.

Good to know

  • It is the clock. Same format as GNOME's (date, weekday, seconds, 12 or 24 hours), and turning Cartouche off gives the old clock back exactly.
  • Out of the way. Over a fullscreen video, GNOME's own pop-ups show as usual. Do Not Disturb is respected.
  • Private. No account, no analytics. Album art is fetched from where the player says it is, and kept in ~/.cache/cartouche.
  • Every GNOME from 45 to 51, on Fedora, Ubuntu, Debian, Arch and the rest. Settings in the Extensions app.

Why “cartouche”?

In Egyptian hieroglyphs, a cartouche is the oval drawn around a name, tied off with a line at one end, so that it stands out from everything around it. Champollion found his way into the Rosetta Stone by reading the names inside them. This one holds the time, and whatever matters right now.

A GNOME take on Alcove for the Mac. From the maker of Manicule and Festoon.
